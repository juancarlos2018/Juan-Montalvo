# Research Brief — Leasing Solar in Hawaiʻi

## The Problem

Electricity is a major household expense in Hawaiʻi, so I wanted to understand whether solar could help lower my monthly costs. Before adding the new solar lease, my December 2023 electric company bill was $362.59, and I was also paying $136.59 for my original Sunrun system. That brought my total monthly cost to about $499.18.

Today, I pay $136.59 for my original Sunrun system and $217.84 for the new lease, including tax. My most recent electric company bill was only $0.87, bringing my total monthly cost to approximately $355.30. Based on these numbers, I am paying about $144 less per month than I was in December 2023.

My experience made me want to understand whether leasing solar is a good financial decision for other homeowners in Hawaiʻi. I want to compare the long-term costs of leasing solar, buying a system, and continuing to rely on the electric company. I also want to consider how rising electricity prices, lease increases, and household energy use affect the overall savings. Although solar has lowered my household expenses, I want to use economic research to determine whether the financial benefits outweigh the costs over time.

## My Hypothesis

My hypothesis is that leasing solar can be a good financial decision for homeowners in Hawaiʻi because it may reduce monthly electricity expenses without requiring them to pay the full cost of a solar system upfront. In my case, my total monthly cost for electricity and both Sunrun payments decreased from approximately $499.18 in December 2023 to $355.30 based on my most recent bill. This represents a reduction of about $144 per month, even though I still have two solar payments.

I expect my solar lease to save me money over time compared to relying on the electric company alone, but I need to determine whether the long-term savings will outweigh the lease payments and annual increases over the 25-year contract.

However, I understand that my experience does not prove that leasing is the best option for everyone. Evidence against my hypothesis could include research showing that purchasing a solar system provides greater long-term savings, that lease payments increase over time, or that some households do not produce enough solar energy to offset their electricity costs. My new lease has a 25-year term and an annual payment increase of approximately 2.99%, which must also be considered when calculating the long-term cost. I will compare these factors and use economic evidence to determine whether leasing solar is financially worthwhile for homeowners in Hawaiʻi.
